## 1. The photograph (confirmed)

- **Photographer:** J. Bond Johnson (James Bond Johnson) of the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram*. Houran & Randle say he "had taken six of the seven photographs in General Ramey's office" (JSE 2002, p. 49, citing Randle 2000). Rudiak, in his *Fortean Times* rebuttal, calls him "the Fort Worth Star-Telegram reporter who took the picture of Ramey holding the message".
- **Date:** July 8, 1947. The wirephoto was transmitted at 11:59 p.m. that night (Houran & Randle p. 49; UTA post "Deciphering the Ramey Memo", 2017). Rudiak estimates the photo was taken around 5:30 p.m., about two hours after the Roswell AP bulletin.
- **"While he told the press":** a fair compression. The photos come from the Fort Worth press session at which Ramey, Col. Thomas DuBose and weather officer Irving Newton identified the debris as a weather balloon (Wikipedia, *Roswell incident*, citing Goldberg 2001 and Saler et al. 1997). The formal identification was Newton's.
- **"Teletype message":** this is the majority assumption (all capitals, fixed pitch), but the document's origin is disputed. Johnson once said he brought a news-wire bulletin and handed it to Ramey, then retracted that; Barry Greenwood argues it is a civilian wire story. "Probably a teletype" is safer.

## 3. Rudiak "since 1994" (corrected)

The 1994 date comes from the iHeart episode description, which says Rudiak's main area of expertise is the Roswell crash, "which he has been studying since1994". That refers to Roswell generally, not the memo. Evidence on when his memo work began:
- Rudiak: "In 1999, concerted efforts to read the message began." Also: "When people began to take another look at the Ramey message in 1999…"
- Houran & Randle (2002) cite his reading as "Rudiak, 2000".
- Randle, June 2016: "David Rudiak's on-going efforts over the last 15 years".

So his memo work dates from about 1999–2000. That is still more than 25 years, so "for more than twenty-five years" is safe.

## 5. Rudiak's "quantitative" argument (corrected)

The claim exists but was **never published**. In a comment on Kevin Randle's blog (Apr 27, 2016, 11:32 a.m.), Rudiak wrote:

> "I used a computer OCR program employing a pattern matching method called cross-correlation, where the letters in the VICTIMS word were matched in probability against a set of template teletype letters (made of equal size and proportions). Of all proposed words there (VICIMS, VIEWING, FINDING, REMAINS) plus two similar nonsense words used as controls (VIRGINS, VIOLINS), VICTIMS was the standout word in letter probability, comparable in overall letter probability to unanimously agreed-upon other words in the memo, WEATHER BALLOONS and FORT WORTH, TEX. … (I have not published these results.)"

He added that he had not repeated the test on the 2015 UTA scans. It was one of four lines of evidence he listed; the other three were reader consensus (6 of 9), a CAPTCHA crowd test run by skeptic Ross Evans (about 2000 readers favoured V, I, I, M, S), and his website poll.

His published case on roswellproof.com rests on other methods:
- letter counts on a column grid;
- side-by-side comparison against a teletype font he compiled from 1947 teletypes (*Victim_compare*: "judge for yourself… by eye");
- a onelook.com dictionary search for VI\*\*I\*S (*Word_Search*, *Methods*).

Calling the cross-correlation test his "strongest argument" is editorial. Worth noting: Rudiak used FORT WORTH, TEX. and WEATHER BALLOONS as controls, the same known words the video's calibration test uses.

## 6. Houran & Randle (2002)

- **Citation (confirmed):** *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002. Authors: James Houran and Kevin D. Randle. The title in print has quotation marks: "A Message in a Bottle:" Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO Case.
- **Design (confirmed):** 176 self-selected participants, randomly assigned to three groups.
  - Study I (N = 59) was told it was a "military memo that could contain information about the crash, retrieval, and subsequent cover-up of an extraterrestrial craft near Roswell".
  - Study II (N = 58) was told it concerned "the secret testing of the atomic bomb".
  - Study III (N = 59) was asked only to read "a photograph of a document". That is close to, but not literally, "told nothing".
- **Findings (the script's summary is incomplete):**
  - Suggestion shaped content. Roswell-primed readers gave "remains", "crash" and "UFO"; atomic-primed readers gave "flash", "glasses", "atomic" and "laboratory".
  - Suggestion shaped quantity. The primed groups deciphered 4.6 and 4.8 words on average; the blind group deciphered 1.6.
  - **However**, the abstract says "a few words in the same locations… were consistently perceived across the three suggestion conditions and these matched the words identified in previous investigations": FORT WORTH TX (n = 52), STORY (n = 61), WEATHER BALLOONS (n = 27), and LAND (n = 47, the primed groups only).
  - VICTIMS does not appear among the reported words.
  - Caveat: the assistant discarded the individual score sheets. Rudiak has published a critique (*Randle_Houran_Graphics*).
  - The on-screen "→ a different reading" under "told: nothing" overstates: the blind group produced few readings.

**8c. Agreement figures (corrected).**
The script's ~76% and ~41% are Rudiak's own numbers, but the ~76% is mislabelled. His page says:

> "plurality to unanimous consensus on about 76% of the words/letters and strong consensus to unanimous agreement on about 41% of the words/letters (at least 2/3rds of readers agree), with 48% consensus or majority to unanimous reads (at least 5 readers agree)."

His "plurality" means 3–5 of 9–10 readers. So **76% is ≥3 readers, not a majority**; majority (≥5) is **48%**. His colour legend is also internally inconsistent: majority is 6–7 and strong ≥7, while the text uses ≥5 and ≥2/3.

**Independent whole-word recount.** The rule:
- Word slots come from Rudiak's top row of x's on lines 1–9: 74 slots, 330 template characters.
- Each reader's latest version is assigned to slots by column overlap, with one manual fix (Burleson, line 7).
- A slot counts as agreement only if readers give the exact same whole word after normalization: uppercase; quotes, parentheses, "?" and "." removed; words containing `*` or `—` count as no vote; RRAMEY and R RAMEY become RAMEY; DISC and DISK are treated as different words.
- The denominator is always 10.

| Threshold | Words (of 74) | Template chars (of 330) |
|---|---|---|
| ≥3/10 (plurality) | 51 (68.9%) | 222 (67.3%) |
| ≥5/10 | 31 (41.9%) | 134 (40.6%) |
| ≥6/10 (majority) | 21 (28.4%) | 87 (26.4%) |
| ≥7/10 | 16 (21.6%) | 61 (18.5%) |
| ≥8/10 (strong) | 14 (18.9%) | 53 (16.1%) |
| 10/10 | 5 (6.8%) | 17 (5.2%) |

**Variants:**
- Allowing any version and merging DISC with DISK raises ≥6/10 to 32.4% of words; ≥8/10 is unchanged.
- A crude letter-level variant (letters left-aligned within slots, partial credit) gives ≥3: 73.3%, ≥5: 46.4%, ≥6: 34.5%, ≥7: 24.5%, ≥8: 17.6%. This roughly reproduces Rudiak's 76% and 48% but **not** his 41% "strong" figure.

Slot alignment is by column overlap, so treat the figures as ±2–3 words.

Words with 8+ of 10 agreement: THE · OF THE WRECK · TO THE (line 2); AT FORT WORTH, TEX. (line 3); THE (line 4); OF (line 6); WEATHER BALLOONS (line 7). The content-word skeleton that survives a strong threshold is **WRECK, FORT WORTH, TEX., WEATHER BALLOONS**; DISC/DISK reaches 7/10 if the spellings are merged.

Suggested screen text: "Rudiak's tally: at least 3 of 10 readers agree on ~76% of the letters, at least 5 on ~48%. Whole-word recount: 6+ of 10 agree on 28% of words; 8+ on 19%."

## 10. What UTA holds (corrected)

- **UTA's description:** "High Resolution **Microfiche** Scans". "High Dynamic Range photography has been used to harness the range of tones present with negatives… The products of HDR imaging have been processed into working files in the form of image stacks where the interaction among layers has been influenced variously to reduce the visual interference of film grain…, separate out the tones of the paper base…, isolate and ('lift') tonal values of the fonts… Resulting files provide a range of image states."
- **Files:** 23 TIFFs, uploaded 2021-11 according to their file paths.
  - Right half: `R Scanpro 55B…85B 800 dpi 2x.tif`, 7 files.
  - Left half: `L Scanpro 55B…85B 800 dpi 2x.tif`, 7 files.
  - Full memo: `All-55B…95B 1000 dpi.tif`, 9 files.
- **Collection:** UTA Libraries Special Collections. The photographs and negatives are "part of the large Fort Worth Star-Telegram collection".
- **Provenance:** the scans were made at UTA in late April 2015. Martin Dreyer organized the session with UTA's Brenda McClurkin, and David Rudiak and forensic-photography volunteers took part (Randle blog, Sept 2015; UTA posts, 2017).
- **What this means for the script:**
  - "High-resolution scans" is fine.
  - Scene 02 says "The scans are nine TIFF files… each captured at four scanner brightness settings". UTA posts **23** files, 7 per half, and describes them as *processed HDR working files* ("image states"), not raw captures at scanner brightness settings. The "B" suffixes may be brightness levels, but UTA does not say so.
  - The high 90%+ correlation and identical grain across files is consistent with the files sharing a single source capture. This is worth stating if the analysis treats them as independent exposures.
